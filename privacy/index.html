Tempus

Privacy Policy

Last updated 15 September 2026

Tempus keeps a detailed record of when you work and what you are trying to become. That record stays yours. In fact almost nothing leaves your devices at all — this page says exactly what does, and how to stop it.

Who is responsible

Tempus is made by Muhammad Ahnaf Abid, an individual developer. For anything on this page — questions, corrections, or a request to delete your data — write to muh.ahnafabid@gmail.com.

Your work never reaches me

Your goals, tasks, projects, notes, estimates and every logged block are stored on your device and synchronised through your own iCloud private database. That is Apple's infrastructure, tied to your Apple Account. I have no access to it, no copy of it, and no way to obtain one.

The same is true of your calendar. Tempus reads your calendars through Apple's EventKit on the device, and events it creates are written into a calendar you choose. None of that passes through any server of mine.

If you use the shield, the apps and websites you pick are represented by opaque tokens issued by Apple's Screen Time system. Those tokens never leave the device that created them, and they mean nothing anywhere else — Tempus is never told which apps you chose.

What does leave your device

Your account, your subscription record, anonymous usage statistics, and your email address if you ask for updates. Never your work.

Your account

Signing in with Apple or Google creates an account held by Supabase, which hosts Tempus's server. It stores an account identifier and the email address your provider gives us. If you use Sign in with Apple and choose to hide your address, we receive only Apple's private relay address and never your real one.

The account exists so your subscription belongs to you rather than to a device. It holds no goals, tasks or blocks, and there is no mechanism by which it could.

Your subscription

Payment is handled entirely by Apple — Tempus never sees your card, your name or your billing address. When you subscribe, Apple gives the app a signed receipt, which Tempus sends to its own server. The server checks the signature with Apple and records whether your subscription is active, which product it is for, and when it expires, against your account.

The mailing list

If you fill in the optional "Occasional updates" field in Settings, that email address is stored on your account record at Supabase, alongside the date you gave it. It is used for nothing but occasional notes about Tempus. Leaving it blank changes nothing about how the app works — and the address you sign in with is never used for those notes, only an address you typed into that field. Deleting your account removes it with everything else.

Written insights stay here too

Tempus works out where your hours went on your device, then writes those findings up in plain English using Apple's own on-device model. No key, no server, no network request. The sentences are composed on the device you are holding and never travel.

This needs Apple Intelligence, which means an iPhone 15 Pro or newer, or a Mac with Apple silicon. Where it is unavailable, Tempus shows the same findings as plain statements instead and says why.

An earlier design sent those sentences to Google's Gemini to be reworded. That was removed on 15 September 2026 before Tempus was ever released, because the sentences carry the names of your goals and projects and no wording of a consent screen made that a good trade. Nothing has ever been sent to Google, because the app was never published in that form.

Anonymous usage statistics (on by default, one switch to stop it)

Tempus uses PostHog to understand which screens get used and where people get stuck. What travels is event names, counts, and figures your device has already worked out for itself — your calibration score, how many blocks you finished, whether you hit the day's target.

What never travels is anything you typed: no task titles, no project names, no goal titles, no notes, no calendar event titles. This is enforced by an automated test that reads the source of every tracking call and fails the build if one carries a title, a name or a note.

Turn it off with "Share anonymous usage" in Settings. Data goes to PostHog in the United States.

What Tempus does not do

  • No tracking. Nothing about you is combined with data from other companies' apps or websites, for advertising or for anything else.
  • No advertising, and no advertising identifiers.
  • No data brokers. Nothing is sold, rented or shared for anyone else's marketing.
  • No profile building. The account is an identifier and an email address. That is the whole of it.

How long things are kept

Your account and subscription record are kept while your account exists, and are deleted when you delete it. Usage statistics are retained by PostHog under their standard retention. Your own work is kept by your iCloud account for as long as you keep it, and is deleted when you delete it — by you, not by me.

Deleting your data

In the app: Settings → Account → Delete account. This permanently deletes your account and your subscription record from Tempus's server. It cannot be undone.

It does not delete your own work, because your work is not mine to delete. Your goals, tasks and blocks live in your own iCloud. Delete the app and remove its data from iCloud in your device's settings to remove those.

Usage statistics: turn off "Share anonymous usage" in Settings and nothing further is sent. To have what was already sent removed, write to the address below.

Cancelling a subscription is done through your Apple Account settings, not here.

You can also write to muh.ahnafabid@gmail.com and ask for deletion, or for a copy of what the account holds.

Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to see what is held about you, to have it corrected or deleted, to object to processing, or to receive a copy. Tempus holds almost nothing — the anonymous usage statistics are the only thing, and the switch in Settings stops them. Write to the address above for anything else and you will get an answer.

Children

Tempus is not directed at children and collects nothing knowingly from them.

Changes

If this policy changes in a way that affects what is collected, the date at the top changes and the new version appears here before the change ships.
